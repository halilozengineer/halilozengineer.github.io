Introduction

Did you know that just 14 millimeters of excessive vertical deflection in an overhead crane can jeopardize an entire industrial facility and put operators at severe risk?

In this case study, we examine step by step how we tested the structural limits of a 24-ton capacity, 12-meter span double-girder overhead crane in ANSYS Workbench, reduced vertical deflection by more than half through geometric modifications, and optimized critical load-bearing components.

Engineering & Learning Objectives

  • Master static, modal, and transient (time-domain dynamic) FEA workflows for heavy industrial overhead cranes.
  • Develop geometric modifications leveraging area moment of inertia to bring excessive deflection within permissible limits.
  • Select alloy steels such as AISI 4140 and AISI 4340 based on FEA stress outputs to satisfy a 7x Factor of Safety (FoS).
  • Optimize finite element mesh quality parameters (Element Quality, Aspect Ratio, Skewness) for high numerical accuracy.

Project Summary

This project investigates the static strength, modal vibration characteristics, and transient dynamic load responses of a double-girder overhead crane in ANSYS Workbench. In initial static analyses under full load, the 12-meter span exhibited an unacceptable 26.4 mm vertical deflection. By integrating a reinforcement girder beneath the main beams, deflection was successfully brought down to the 12 mm (L/1000) standard limit. Based on stress distributions, AISI 4140 alloy steel was assigned to the main girders and AISI 4340 to the hoist shafts, guaranteeing an industrial 7x safety factor.

Problem Definition

In heavy-duty bridge cranes, the most critical design parameter is vertical deflection. According to national and international crane engineering standards, the maximum permissible vertical deflection of bridge girders is one-thousandth of the span length (L / 1000).

For our 12-meter (12,000 mm) bridge span, this limit is strictly 12 mm. However, under a nominal payload of 24 tons (235,440 N), our baseline design exhibited ~26.4 mm of vertical deflection. Because excessive flexing can induce structural instability, fatigue cracking, and trolley derailment, structural revision was mandatory.

Approach & Design Process

Computer-Aided Engineering (CAE) processes were systematically applied to resolve the deflection bottleneck:

  1. CAD Defeaturing: Small components that do not influence global structural stiffness—such as cables, bolts, chains, and buffers—were removed to optimize mesh generation and solver runtime.
  2. Mesh Optimization: A tetrahedral mesh was generated with dedicated Body Sizing and Face Sizing on critical load zones, achieving high mesh quality metrics:
    • Average Element Quality: 0.835 (Well above the minimum 0.70 target)
    • Aspect Ratio: 1.86 (Close to the ideal value of 1.0)
    • Skewness: 0.22 (Close to the ideal value of 0, ensuring smooth element transitions)
    • Total Nodes: 457,011 | Total Elements: 236,475
  3. Structural Modification: To reduce deflection from 26.4 mm to under 12 mm, classical bending stress and area moment of inertia equations were utilized:
$$ \sigma = \frac{M \cdot c}{I} $$
$$ I = \frac{b \cdot h^3}{12} $$

Because the area moment of inertia (\( I \)) for a rectangular cross-section scales cubically with height (\( h^3 \)), an additional reinforcement beam oriented vertically along the Z-axis was integrated beneath the main girders, and critical hoist shaft diameters were doubled.

Results & Outputs

Final FEA simulations following the geometric reinforcements confirmed that all structural targets were met:

  • Final Deflection (54% Improvement): With the reinforcement beam and alloy steel assignment, vertical deformation was reduced from 26.4 mm down to the 12 mm permissible limit.
  • Girder Stress & Material Selection: Maximum bending stress on the upper girder flange was recorded at 65 MPa. To satisfy the 7x safety factor required for heavy industrial cranes (\( 65\text{ MPa} \times 7 = 455\text{ MPa} \) minimum yield strength), high-strength AISI 4140 alloy steel was selected.
  • Hoist Shafts & Shear Stress: Maximum combined torsional and shear stress on the critical hoist shafts reached 145.8–160 MPa. To maintain a 7x safety factor, AISI 4340 alloy steel was assigned to the shafts.
  • Modal Vibration Behavior: The first three natural frequencies were mapped at 8.11 Hz, 12.14 Hz, and 29.34 Hz, preventing resonance overlap with motor operating speeds.
  • Transient (Dynamic) Analysis: Time-domain simulations verified that stresses ramp smoothly during the initial 5-second lifting phase and remain steady during constant-velocity horizontal trolley travel.

Summary

In this study, structural verification and FEA optimization of a double-girder industrial overhead crane operating under demanding heavy-industry constraints were successfully completed in ANSYS Workbench.

Key Takeaways

  • Geometric Reinforcement: By increasing the area moment of inertia via an auxiliary bottom girder, vertical deflection was improved by 54%.
  • Targeted Alloy Selection: Guided by FEA stress contours, AISI 4140 on girders and AISI 4340 on shafts guaranteed a 7x Factor of Safety (FoS).
  • Dynamic Stability & Mesh Accuracy: Modal and transient analyses combined with an 83.5% average element quality confirmed structural reliability against resonance and impact loading.
